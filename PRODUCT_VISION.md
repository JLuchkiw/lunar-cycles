# Lunar Cycles
## Product Vision & Design Principles

**Status:** Initial product vision - subject to iteration  
**Project:** Lunar Cycles  
**Repository:** https://github.com/JLuchkiw/lunar-cycles

---

## 1. Product Vision

Lunar Cycles is a gentle, lunar-inspired personal planner designed to help individuals honor their capacity, celebrate meaningful accomplishments, and pursue personal growth without demanding constant productivity.

By combining practical task management, intention-setting, reflection, and the natural rhythm of lunar cycles, the application encourages users to recognize progress in all its forms.

Lunar Cycles is built around a simple philosophy:

**Growth is cyclical, not linear. Progress is personal, and rest is part of the journey.**

The application aims to provide structure without rigidity, encouragement without judgment, and opportunities for reflection without requiring spiritual beliefs or practices.

## 2. The Problem We're Addressing

Traditional productivity applications frequently emphasize measurable output: completed tasks, maintained streaks, deadlines met, and goals achieved.

While these measurements can be useful, they do not always reflect an individual's circumstances, effort, or personal growth.

An important distinction exists between **ability and capacity**.

An individual may possess the ability to accomplish a task without having the physical, emotional, or cognitive capacity to complete it at a particular moment.

Capacity can fluctuate. Progress can be interrupted. Priorities can change.

These experiences should not automatically be interpreted as failure.

Lunar Cycles seeks to provide an alternative to productivity systems that assume continuous, measurable improvement.

Rather than asking only *"How much did you accomplish?"*, the application encourages users to consider:

- What mattered to me today?
- What was possible within my capacity?
- What did I accomplish, experience, or learn?
- What might I approach differently?
- What am I ready to continue, change, or release?

## 3. Intended User Experience

Lunar Cycles should feel like a welcoming personal space rather than an accountability dashboard.

The experience should be:

- **Calm:** Muted colors, restrained animation, and uncluttered interfaces.
- **Encouraging:** Thoughtful language that recognizes effort without exaggerated praise.
- **Practical:** Everyday task management remains straightforward and accessible.
- **Flexible:** Users can engage with the application as deeply or as simply as they choose.
- **Inclusive:** Lunar symbolism provides an optional reflective framework rather than prescribing spiritual beliefs.
- **Nonjudgmental:** Missed tasks, changed goals, periods of rest, and released intentions do not result in punishment or negative messaging.

Users should be able to open the application, identify what matters to them, and take a meaningful action with minimal friction.

## 4. Core Product Concepts

### 4.1 Tasks - Everyday Accomplishments

Tasks represent individual actions, responsibilities, or activities.

Users should be able to create, organize, schedule, edit, and complete tasks.

Tasks may exist independently or be associated with a larger intention.

The application should recognize that seemingly small accomplishments can represent significant effort.

A completed task is an accomplishment, not merely a contribution toward a productivity score.

### 4.2 Intentions - Personal Direction

Intentions represent values, aspirations, or directions an individual wishes to explore.

Unlike conventional goals, intentions do not necessarily require measurable outcomes or fixed deadlines.

For example:

**Intention:** Make more space for creativity.

**Possible actions:** Sketch for ten minutes, visit a library, or explore a new hobby.

An intention may remain meaningful even when the associated actions change.

Users should be able to:

- Create intentions at any time.
- Maintain ongoing or cycle-specific intentions.
- Associate tasks with intentions when useful.
- Renew, revise, pause, or release intentions.
- Reflect on intentions without being required to demonstrate measurable progress.

Releasing an intention is a valid outcome, not a failure.

Historical intentions and reflections should remain accessible unless deliberately deleted by the user.

### 4.3 Lunar Cycles - A Rhythm, Not a Deadline

The lunar cycle provides an optional framework for periodic reflection and personal growth.

The application may offer prompts associated with four broad phases:

| Phase | Suggested Focus |
|---|---|
| New Moon | Set intentions and explore possibilities |
| Waxing Moon | Experiment, act, and take manageable steps |
| Full Moon | Recognize accomplishments and celebrate growth |
| Waning Moon | Reflect, reassess, rest, and release |

These associations are symbolic prompts, not behavioral requirements or scientific claims about the effects of lunar phases.

Users may create, revise, celebrate, or release intentions during any phase.

**The lunar cycle provides rhythm, not rules.**

### 4.4 Calendar - A Flexible Timeline

The calendar provides a familiar way to navigate dates and organize tasks.

The initial version will prioritize date-associated tasks rather than full appointment and event management.

Tasks displayed on the calendar should share the same underlying data as tasks displayed elsewhere in the application.

Future development may introduce dedicated calendar events, recurring appointments, and external calendar integrations.

### 4.5 Reflection - Recognizing the Journey

Reflection allows users to recognize accomplishments, acknowledge challenges, and explore changes in their priorities or understanding.

Reflection should remain optional and should not require users to justify incomplete tasks.

The application should support recognizing meaningful experiences that cannot be reduced to numerical measurements.

## 5. Design Principles

### Principle 1: Capacity Is Not Ability

The application must not assume that an incomplete task reflects a lack of ability, motivation, or commitment.

Design decisions should accommodate fluctuating capacity without imposing judgment.

### Principle 2: Progress Is Not Always Linear

Growth may involve exploration, repetition, rest, adaptation, setbacks, and changes in direction.

The application should support these experiences rather than treating them as interruptions to an otherwise continuous upward trajectory.

### Principle 3: Encourage Without Pressuring

Language should be gentle, thoughtful, and sincere.

Avoid shame-based notifications, punitive streaks, exaggerated affirmations, and artificial urgency.

Encouragement should never become another obligation.

### Principle 4: Offer Structure Without Rigidity

Users should have access to organizational tools without being required to follow a prescribed workflow.

Lunar prompts, intentions, reflections, and capacity check-ins should remain optional.

### Principle 5: Celebrate Meaningful Accomplishments

Completed tasks, personal discoveries, changing priorities, and intentional rest can all represent meaningful experiences.

The application should support recognition without assigning personal worth to productivity metrics.

### Principle 6: Accessibility Is Foundational

The application should prioritize readable typography, sufficient color contrast, screen-reader compatibility, scalable interfaces, and reduced-motion support.

Accessibility should be considered during initial design rather than added after development.

### Principle 7: Practicality Before Decoration

Visual design should enhance usability rather than obstruct it.

Lunar imagery, animations, and reflective prompts should never unnecessarily delay or complicate routine actions.

### Principle 8: Privacy and User Autonomy

Personal goals, reflections, and capacity-related information may be sensitive.

The application should minimize unnecessary data collection, provide clear user controls, and avoid requiring accounts for functionality that can operate locally.

## 6. Visual and Interaction Direction

The initial design direction emphasizes a minimalist, moonlit aesthetic.

**Visual characteristics:**
- Muted navy, lavender, plum, sage, and neutral tones.
- Subtle celestial imagery and moon-phase illustrations.
- Clear visual hierarchy and uncluttered layouts.
- Calm, readable typography.
- Restrained transitions and optional animations.

**Potential opening experience:**

A minimal night-sky-inspired splash screen featuring a moon-phase animation when loading is necessary.

The home screen should present a lunar-phase indicator, a gentle greeting, and prominently accessible daily tasks.

The interface should feel whimsical without becoming overly decorative or distracting.

## 7. Initial MVP Scope

The first functional version will prioritize:

| Area | Initial Functionality |
|---|---|
| Home | Moon phase, greeting, and daily tasks |
| Tasks | Create, edit, complete, and assign dates |
| Calendar | Navigate dates and view associated tasks |
| Intentions | Create, edit, pause, renew, and release intentions |
| Connections | Optionally associate tasks with intentions |
| Reflection | Create and review short, dated reflections |
| History | Review accomplishments and past intentions |
| Persistence | Store user data locally |

**Deferred functionality:**

- User accounts and cloud synchronization.
- External calendar integration.
- Automated capacity-based task suggestions.
- Social and community features.
- Advanced gamification.
- Complex notification systems.

The MVP should validate the application's core experience before introducing additional complexity.

## 8. Long-Term Direction

Future development may expand Lunar Cycles into a more comprehensive personal planning environment.

Potential enhancements include cross-device synchronization, recurring routines, calendar integrations, customizable reflection prompts, optional capacity-aware recommendations, and additional accessibility features.

These possibilities are not commitments.

New features should be evaluated against the product's central purpose: helping individuals pursue meaningful growth while respecting their capacity and autonomy.

## 9. Guiding Question

When evaluating a new feature, design decision, or interaction, ask:

**Does this help the user recognize their capacity, honor their intentions, and appreciate their progress — or does it create another expectation they must satisfy?**

Lunar Cycles should be a tool that supports the individual, never another standard against which they must measure themselves.
